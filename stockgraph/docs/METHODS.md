## 1. Pearson Correlation & Rolling Dynamic Correlation

### What is it in plain English?
Correlation measures how closely the price movements of two stocks move in harmony. If Apple goes up and Microsoft almost always goes up on the same day, their correlation is close to $+1.0$. If one moves completely opposite to another (like an airline stock and an oil producer during an oil price spike), their correlation is negative.

### Mathematical Formulation
Given daily returns $R_X$ and $R_Y$ over $T$ days:
$$\rho_{X, Y} = \frac{\text{Cov}(R_X, R_Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T (R_{X,t} - \bar{R}_X)(R_{Y,t} - \bar{R}_Y)}{\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^T (R_{X,t} - \bar{R}_X)^2} \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^T (R_{Y,t} - \bar{R}_Y)^2}}$$

### Rolling Correlation
Financial markets are non-stationary: two stocks that were uncorrelated in 2013 might become tightly coupled during the 2016-2017 tech rally. Rolling correlation slides a fixed window (e.g., 60 days) day-by-day across time:
$$\rho_t(w) = \text{Corr}(R_{X, [t-w+1 : t]}, R_{Y, [t-w+1 : t]})$$

### How to explain in Viva:
> *"We do not compute correlation on raw price levels because prices are non-stationary and trend upward, which would create spurious correlation. Instead, we compute correlation strictly on daily percentage log/arithmetic returns. A rolling 60-day window reveals how cross-stock dependencies dynamically evolve across different market regimes."*

## 2. Market Beta & Jensen's Alpha (CAPM)

### What is it in plain English?
- **Beta ($\beta$)** measures how sensitive a stock is to broad market movements. If the S&P 500 moves by $1\%$, a high-beta stock ($\beta = 1.5$) typically swings by $1.5\%$. A low-beta defensive stock ($\beta = 0.5$, like a utility company) only swings by $0.5\%$.
- **Alpha ($\alpha$)** is the "excess return" generated by the stock above what was expected given its risk (beta). Positive alpha means the stock outperformed its risk-adjusted benchmark.

### Mathematical Formulation
$$\beta_i = \frac{\text{Cov}(R_i, R_M)}{\text{Var}(R_M)}$$
$$\alpha_i = R_{i, \text{ann}} - \left(r_f + \beta_i (R_{M, \text{ann}} - r_f)\right)$$
Where:
- $R_M$ is the equal-weight market index return built from all kept S&P 500 stocks.
- $r_f$ is the risk-free rate (default $2\%$ annualized, representing US Treasury yields).

### How to explain in Viva:
> *"Beta answers: 'Is this stock an amplifier or a dampener of market shocks?' In our contagion engine, beta scales the shock transmitted from a seed stock to its peers, capturing leverage and operating sensitivity."*

## 3. Annualized Return (CAGR) & Annualized Volatility

### What is it in plain English?
- **CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)** smooths out multi-year price growth into an equivalent constant yearly interest rate.
- **Annualized Volatility** measures how erratic or noisy the daily fluctuations are over a standard trading year (252 trading days).

### Mathematical Formulation
$$\text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{P_{\text{last}}}{P_{\text{first}}}\right)^{\frac{252}{T}} - 1$$
$$\sigma_{\text{ann}} = \sigma_{\text{daily}} \times \sqrt{252} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T-1} \sum_{t=1}^T (R_t - \bar{R})^2} \times \sqrt{252}$$

### Why $\sqrt{252}$?
Under the assumption of independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) returns, variance scales linearly with time: $\text{Var}(R_{\text{year}}) = 252 \times \text{Var}(R_{\text{day}})$. Since volatility is standard deviation (the square root of variance), $\sigma_{\text{year}} = \sqrt{252} \times \sigma_{\text{day}}$.

## 4. Sharpe Ratio

### What is it in plain English?
The Sharpe ratio measures **return per unit of risk**. A stock with $20\%$ return and $40\%$ volatility has the same Sharpe ratio ($0.45$) as a stock with $11\%$ return and $20\%$ volatility. Investors prefer higher Sharpe ratios because they deliver better returns without stomach-churning roller-coaster drops.

### Mathematical Formulation
$$\text{Sharpe Ratio} = \frac{R_{\text{ann}} - r_f}{\sigma_{\text{ann}}}$$
- $\text{Sharpe} > 1.0$: Considered good in quantitative asset management.
- $\text{Sharpe} > 2.0$: Very strong / institutional quality.
- $\text{Sharpe} < 0$: The asset failed to even beat the risk-free cash rate.

## 5. Maximum Drawdown (Underwater Dynamics)

### What is it in plain English?
Maximum Drawdown answers the worst-case question: **"If I bought at the absolute peak and sold at the absolute worst bottom before recovery, what percentage of my money would I have lost?"**

### Mathematical Formulation
For any day $t$:
$$\text{Peak}_t = \max_{s \le t} P_s$$
$$\text{Drawdown}_t = \frac{P_t - \text{Peak}_t}{\text{Peak}_t}$$
$$\text{Max Drawdown} = \min_{t} \text{Drawdown}_t$$

### How to explain in Viva:
> *"Unlike volatility (which penalizes upside surges equally with downside crashes), drawdown isolates strictly downside wealth destruction. A strategy with a Sharpe of 1.5 but a -60% max drawdown is dangerous because most investors will panic-sell during the crash."*

## 6. Relative Strength Index (RSI - 14-Day Wilder's Smoothing)

### What is it in plain English?
RSI is an oscillator between $0$ and $100$ that measures the momentum and velocity of price changes over the last 14 days.
- $\text{RSI} \ge 70$: **Overbought** (price surged too fast; mean-reversion pullbacks become likely).
- $\text{RSI} \le 30$: **Oversold** (price crashed too hard; potential bargain buying rebound).

### Mathematical Formulation
1. Daily price difference: $\Delta P_t = P_t - P_{t-1}$.
2. Upward gain: $U_t = \max(\Delta P_t, 0)$, Downward loss: $D_t = \max(-\Delta P_t, 0)$.
3. Wilder's Exponential Smoothing (decay factor $\alpha = \frac{1}{14}$):
   $$\text{AvgGain}_t = \frac{13 \times \text{AvgGain}_{t-1} + U_t}{14}$$
   $$\text{AvgLoss}_t = \frac{13 \times \text{AvgLoss}_{t-1} + D_t}{14}$$
4. Relative Strength: $\text{RS} = \frac{\text{AvgGain}_t}{\text{AvgLoss}_t}$.
5. Final index:
   $$\text{RSI}_t = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + \text{RS}_t}$$

## 7. Network Centrality: Degree & Betweenness Centrality

In StockGraph, the market is modeled as an undirected weighted graph $G = (V, E)$, where vertices $V$ are stocks, and edges $E$ connect pairs whose return correlation exceeds a threshold $\rho \ge \tau$.

### Degree Centrality ($k_i$)
- **Meaning:** The total number of other stocks directly connected to stock $i$.
- **Financial interpretation:** Highly interconnected hub stocks (e.g. JPMorgan, Apple, Microsoft) that move closely with many peers.

### Betweenness Centrality ($C_B(v)$)
- **Meaning:** The fraction of all shortest paths between all pairs in the network that pass through node $v$:
  $$C_B(v) = \sum_{s \ne v \ne t} \frac{\sigma_{st}(v)}{\sigma_{st}}$$
  Where $\sigma_{st}$ is the total number of shortest paths from node $s$ to node $t$, and $\sigma_{st}(v)$ is the number of those paths that pass through $v$.
- **Financial interpretation:** **Systemic Bridge Stocks.** These stocks sit at the boundary between two disparate economic sectors (e.g., General Electric bridging Industrials and Finance, or Amazon bridging Consumer Retail and Cloud Technology). If a shock hits one sector, it must traverse bridge stocks with high betweenness to contaminate the rest of the market.

## 8. Community Detection: Greedy Modularity Maximization

### What is it in plain English?
Without looking at sector labels, can an algorithm automatically group stocks that behave alike?
**Clauset-Newman-Moore Greedy Modularity** does exactly this: it starts with every stock in its own cluster and iteratively merges clusters that maximize the graph's **Modularity score ($Q$)**.

### Mathematical Formulation
$$Q = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{i,j} \left[ A_{ij} - \frac{k_i k_j}{2m} \right] \delta(c_i, c_j)$$
Where:
- $A_{ij}$ is the adjacency weight between stock $i$ and $j$.
- $k_i, k_j$ are node degrees, and $m = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{ij} A_{ij}$ is total edge weight.
- $\frac{k_i k_j}{2m}$ is the expected connection probability under a random null model.
- $\delta(c_i, c_j) = 1$ if stocks $i$ and $j$ belong to the same community, else $0$.

### Viva Key Insight:
> *"In our empirical results, greedy modularity spontaneously discovers real-world industrial sectors (Financials group together, Tech giants group together, Big Pharma groups together) purely from correlation patterns without any supervised training!"*

## 9. Unsupervised Market Regimes: Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM)

### What is it in plain English?
Financial markets oscillate between distinct macro states:
1. **Calm:** Low volatility, low cross-correlation, healthy sector dispersion.
2. **Normal:** Typical day-to-day market dynamics.
3. **Stressed / Contagion:** High volatility, spikes in pairwise correlation (where "everything correlates to 1.0" in a panic selloff).

### Why GMM over K-Means?
K-Means assumes circular (spherical) clusters with equal variance. In financial data, correlation and volatility are positively co-dependent and have elliptical, elongated distributions with probabilistic uncertainty. GMM fits a mixture of 3 multivariate Gaussian distributions:
$$p(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^3 \pi_k \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_k, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k)$$
Where $\mathbf{x}_t = [\sigma_{\text{vol}, 20}(t), \bar{\rho}_{20}(t)]^T$ is the 2D feature vector on day $t$.

## 10. Epidemic Network Contagion Simulation Model

### What is it in plain English?
When a major firm experiences a catastrophic failure (e.g., Lehman Brothers in 2008, or Apple issuing an unexpected iPhone revenue warning), the shock doesn't stay confined. It cascades through direct suppliers and peers, and then ripples outward through secondary and tertiary links.

### The Multi-Hop Propagation Algorithm
1. **Hop 0 (Seed Shock):**
   Seed stock $S$ receives initial shock $I_S^{(0)} = \Delta$.
2. **Hop 1 (Direct Contagion):**
   For all stocks $j$ where $\rho_{S, j} \ge \tau$:
   $$\Delta I_j^{(1)} = I_S^{(0)} \times \rho_{S, j} \times \beta_{j|S} \times \delta^1$$
   Where:
   - $\rho_{S, j}$ is the historical correlation.
   - $\beta_{j|S} = \frac{\text{Cov}(S, j)}{\text{Var}(S)}$ is the relative transmission elasticity (clipped to $[0.1, 3.0]$).
   - $\delta \in (0, 1]$ is the damping factor (attenuation per hop, default $0.70$).
3. **Hop $h$ (Cascading Cascade):**
   $$\Delta I_k^{(h)} = \sum_{j \in \text{Frontier}^{(h-1)}} \Delta I_j^{(h-1)} \times \rho_{j, k} \times \beta_{k|j} \times \delta^h$$
4. **Total Cumulative Impact:**
   $$\text{TotalImpact}_k = \sum_{h=0}^H \Delta I_k^{(h)}$$

## 11. Zero Look-Ahead Bias in Quantitative Backtesting

### What is Look-Ahead Bias?
Look-ahead bias is the cardinal sin of algorithmic trading: using information that was not yet known at the time the decision was made (e.g., using day $t$'s closing price to calculate correlation, or using future next-day returns to select stocks).

### How StockGraph Prevents It:
1. **Correlation Estimation Window:** Computed strictly over $[t - \text{lookback}, t - 1]$. The return on day $t$ is NEVER touched when calculating neighbor correlations.
2. **Peer Signal Generation:** For each stock $i$, its signal at $t - 1$ close is the average return of its top-$k$ historical peers on day $t-1$.
3. **Execution & Realization:** The portfolio weights are formed at $t - 1$ close. They enter the market on day $t$ and earn $R_i(t)$.
4. **Transaction Costs:** Turnover fees ($cost\_bps$) are deducted from the daily gross return.

## 12. Common Viva Questions & Ready Answers

### Q1: "Why did you use Parquet instead of CSV for the processed data?"
> **Answer:** *"Parquet is a columnar binary storage format with snappy/gzip compression. In our pipeline, it reduced raw storage from ~30MB CSV to compact Parquet files, allowing sub-second columnar reads of wide price and return matrices into memory."*

### Q2: "Why do you drop tickers with less than 90% coverage?"
> **Answer:** *"Stocks that IPO'd late (like PayPal in 2015) or were delisted lack data for the early years of the 2013-2018 window. Including them would introduce artificial zeros or NaN columns into our correlation matrix, causing non-positive semidefinite matrices and distortion in network topology."*

### Q3: "What happens to the correlation network during a market crash?"
> **Answer:** *"During market panics, cross-stock correlations spike drastically towards 1.0 (the 'diversification breakdown' phenomenon). In our network view, if the user tests a high threshold like 0.7 during calm periods, the graph is sparse; during stressed GMM regimes, dense edge clusters emerge."*

### Q4: "Why did your peer momentum strategy show short-term reversal?"
> **Answer:** *"In our 1-day horizon backtest, stocks whose peers surged on day $t-1$ often experience mild short-term mean reversion on day $t$. This aligns with empirical market microstructure literature (Jegadeesh & Titman; Avellaneda & Lee), where daily lead-lag rebalancing exhibits liquidity provision dynamics."*

## 13. Technical Indicators Mathematical Formulations

### Bollinger Bands (20-day, $2\sigma$)
- **Middle Band:** 20-day Simple Moving Average (SMA):
  $$\text{SMA}_{20}(t) = \frac{1}{20} \sum_{i=0}^{19} P(t-i)$$
- **Upper Band:** $\text{Upper}(t) = \text{SMA}_{20}(t) + 2 \cdot \sigma_{20}(t)$
- **Lower Band:** $\text{Lower}(t) = \text{SMA}_{20}(t) - 2 \cdot \sigma_{20}(t)$
- **Bandwidth:** $\text{Bandwidth} = \frac{\text{Upper} - \text{Lower}}{\text{Middle}}$
- **%B (Percent B):** $\%B = \frac{P - \text{Lower}}{\text{Upper} - \text{Lower}}$

### Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD 12, 26, 9)
- **Fast EMA (12):** $\text{EMA}_{12}(t) = \alpha_{12} P(t) + (1 - \alpha_{12}) \text{EMA}_{12}(t-1)$ where $\alpha = \frac{2}{N+1} = \frac{2}{13}$
- **Slow EMA (26):** $\text{EMA}_{26}(t) = \alpha_{26} P(t) + (1 - \alpha_{26}) \text{EMA}_{26}(t-1)$ where $\alpha = \frac{2}{27}$
- **MACD Line:** $\text{MACD}(t) = \text{EMA}_{12}(t) - \text{EMA}_{26}(t)$
- **Signal Line:** 9-day EMA of the MACD line: $\text{Signal}(t) = \text{EMA}_9(\text{MACD}(t))$
- **Histogram:** $\text{Histogram}(t) = \text{MACD}(t) - \text{Signal}(t)$

### Wilder's Smoothing RSI (14-day)
- Uses exponential smoothing with $\alpha = 1/14$ rather than simple moving averages:
  $$\text{AvgGain}(t) = \frac{\text{AvgGain}(t-1) \cdot 13 + \text{Gain}(t)}{14}$$
  $$\text{AvgLoss}(t) = \frac{\text{AvgLoss}(t-1) \cdot 13 + \text{Loss}(t)}{14}$$
  $$\text{RS}(t) = \frac{\text{AvgGain}(t)}{\text{AvgLoss}(t)}, \quad \text{RSI}(t) = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + \text{RS}(t)}$$

## 14. Real-Time Streaming & Market Microstructure

### Alpaca IEX vs Consolidated SIP
- **Investors Exchange (IEX):** The free Alpaca tier streams real-time trade and quote updates directly from the IEX exchange venue.
- **Microstructure Characteristics:** While IEX represents ~2-4% of total US equity market volume, price discovery is tightly co-integrated with the Consolidated SIP.
- **In-Memory Candle Aggregator:**
  - Raw trade ticks arrive with `(timestamp, price, size)`.
  - The aggregator maps each tick to a 1-minute bucket: $\lfloor \text{unix\_ts} / 60 \rfloor \times 60$.
  - First tick sets `open`, running minimum sets `low`, running maximum sets `high`, latest tick updates `close`, and size increments cumulative `volume`.
  - When the minute rolls over, the candle is finalized and added to a 5-minute rolling candle accumulator.
  - Bounded memory protection ensures each symbol retains at most 2,000 candles in a high-efficiency double-ended queue (`collections.deque`).

## 15. AI Agent Grounding & Tool Calling Architecture

### Grounding Doctrine
To prevent large language model hallucinations:
1. **Schema-Defined Internal Functions:** The backend exposes 7 quantitative functions as JSON schemas (`get_analytics`, `get_network`, `run_contagion_simulation`, `run_backtest`, `get_regimes`, `get_anomalies`, `get_live_status`).
2. **Tool Dispatcher Loop:** When a user poses a question in the "Ask the Market" drawer, the model outputs tool calls. The server executes the function against the in-memory dataset and returns pure JSON numbers back to the LLM.
3. **Citation Requirement:** The model is constrained via system prompt instructions to state the source tool for every figure.
4. **Compliance Assurance:** Automatically appends educational disclaimers; provides no personalized investment advice.

## 16. Further Viva Defense Questions

### Q5: "How does the frontend achieve 60fps with real-time updates?"
> **Answer:** *"We use two key techniques: First, `@tanstack/react-virtual` renders only the 10-12 visible DOM nodes for the 482 S&P 500 stocks instead of mounting all 482 nodes into the DOM. Second, for the candlestick chart, we use TradingView's `lightweight-charts` with `series.update()` to mutate only the active bar on HTML5 Canvas instead of re-rendering React component trees."*

### Q6: "Why batch WebSocket updates at 250ms instead of streaming every raw tick?"
> **Answer:** *"In high-frequency feeds, hundreds of ticks can arrive per second. Flooding the browser with unthrottled postMessages saturates the single-threaded JavaScript event loop and causes UI stutter. Batching into frames at $\le 4$ fps (250ms) preserves human visual perception limits (60Hz monitor refresh rates) while keeping CPU utilization below 2%."*
